# Perfect Competition: Engagement Brief

## The problem

The farm must determine how to allocate its 64 available planting beds among tomatoes, carrots, and mesclun to maximize seasonal profit. Each crop generates different revenue and requires different amounts of labor. Although tomatoes generate the highest revenue per bed, their labor requirements increase more rapidly as production expands. The farm must also account for fertilizer expenses, available labor, temporary worker costs, and the maximum number of beds allowed for each crop. The challenge is finding a planting combination that generates sufficient revenue without allowing operating costs to reduce profitability.

## What I am assuming

I am assuming that the highest-revenue crop will not necessarily generate the highest overall profit because labor requirements and diminishing returns affect production costs. I also assume that diversifying the planting allocation will help balance revenue and expenses rather than concentrating heavily on one crop. Since the farm has limited resources, I believe leaving some beds unplanted could be more profitable than using all available land if the additional production costs exceed the revenue generated.

## Hypothesis

I predict that allocating 18 beds to tomatoes, 18 beds to carrots, and 25 beds to mesclun will provide a profitable balance between revenue and operating costs. This combination uses 61 of the 64 available beds, leaving three unplanted.

I would allocate 18 beds to tomatoes because they generate the highest revenue per bed, but I expect their rapidly increasing labor requirements to make planting the maximum 20 beds less attractive. Carrots require relatively little labor, which should help control expenses. Mesclun provides additional revenue while experiencing a relatively low rate of diminishing returns. I expect this combination to balance revenue potential with labor costs and generate a strong seasonal profit.

## How I would know I was wrong

I would know my hypothesis was incorrect if the Excel model identifies a different planting combination that generates greater seasonal profit. For example, the model might show that planting all 20 tomato beds is profitable despite their increasing labor requirements, or that allocating more beds to mesclun and fewer to carrots would improve overall profit.

I will compare my predicted allocation with the model's results and examine how revenue, labor costs, and diminishing returns affect the optimal planting decision. This comparison will help determine whether my initial assumptions were reasonable and identify which factors I underestimated or overestimated.
